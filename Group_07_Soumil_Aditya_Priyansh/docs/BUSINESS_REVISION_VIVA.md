# Business revision for the live demo and viva

Revision date: 30 October 2026. The existing 03:23.73 video remains unchanged and shows the original conservative business case. The revised case is a later prospective scenario, not a correction to the frozen robot experiments.

## Soumil's explanation

“Our video uses the original conservative case: 7.5 rooms per day, a full manual cleanup after any incomplete robot attempt, and no automatic conversion of released staff time into cash. That case has no finite payback.

“We subsequently examined a partial-cleanup workflow. Across the same 50 proposed trials, 663 of 800 objects were correctly placed, while only 28 of 50 rooms were fully cleared. Those are different measures; the robot's full-room success remains 56 percent.

“Our revised scenario assumes that a person finishes the remaining objects, with equal human effort per object and an additional 30-second inspection in every room. It also assumes 15 rooms per day over a 12-hour dispatch window and that all released labour value genuinely avoids paid expenditure. We have not measured those hospital assumptions.

“Under those conditions, the calculation releases 31.49 staff-minutes per day, reduces annual cash cost by 7.81 percent and gives simple payback of 44.54 months, within the assumed five-year life. If only 75 percent of the labour value becomes cash savings, payback grows to 79.83 months, beyond that life. With no spending avoided, there is no payback.

“The robot is still slower for room readiness: 26.59 minutes versus an assumed manual 15.2 minutes. We therefore present a conditional staff-assistance business case, not faster bed turnover, proven profit or clinical effectiveness.”
